Questions

What ages is this for?

Built for 2–5. The youngest target is 2-year-olds who can point a camera; the oldest is pre-K kids learning shapes, colors, and early vocabulary. The audio-first design and giant tap targets are sized for that range.

Does it use my kid's photos?

No. Photos go to Google's Gemini Vision API for one-time matching, then disappear. Nothing is stored, nothing is trained on, nothing is shared. The app itself stores zero personal data — there's no signup, no account, no email collection. Progress lives in your browser.

Will the AI always get it right?

Mostly. Modern vision AI is impressive but not perfect — and a 3-year-old's photos are particularly hard (blurry, off-center, weird angles). We tuned the AI prompts to be permissive (a sippy cup counts as a cup, a stuffed lion counts as a lion). When it does get it wrong, you can tap the parent override to mark it correct. Better to err on celebration than frustration.

Does it work offline?

The app shell loads offline (great for spotty WiFi). The AI photo-matching needs internet — that part can't work offline. If your kid takes a photo while you're traveling, they'll get a friendly "no internet" message.

How is this different from Khan Academy Kids or Sago Mini?

Those are great apps but they're tap-the-screen. Picture Hunt is the only app we know of that combines AI camera validation with a toddler-first UX and a real learning curriculum. Your kid actually moves through their physical environment — that's the entire point.

Why not on the App Store?

For now, it runs in your browser and installs to your home screen as a "PWA" (progressive web app). It feels like a native app once installed. We may build a proper iOS/Android app if enough parents subscribe — that's the ladder we're climbing.

Who built this?

One dad, one 3-year-old, one coffee. The 3-year-old loved it. We're seeing if other parents do too.
